## Overview

Footers are used to provide additional information about the website or application, such as
copyright information, links to social media profiles, and contact information. They are typically
located at the bottom of the page and can be designed to be visually distinct from the main content.
SEO best practices suggest that footers should include important links and information that users
may need to access frequently, such as contact information, privacy policies, and terms of service.
Additionally, footers can be used to improve the overall user experience by providing easy access to
important information and resources.

### When to Use Footers

Footers should be used when you want to provide users with consistent access to important information and links at the bottom of every page. They are particularly useful for:

- Displaying company information and branding.
- Providing quick links to key sections of the website.
- Offering contact details and support options.
- Linking to social media profiles.
- Presenting legal information such as privacy policies and terms of service.

By including a well-structured footer, you can enhance the user experience and ensure that essential information is easily accessible. Typical usages would be on corporate websites, e-commerce platforms, blogs, and informational websites where users benefit from having consistent access to key information and links at the bottom of each page.

### When Not to Use Footers

Footers should be avoided when they would add unnecessary clutter to the page or when the information they contain is not relevant to the user. Overloading a page with footer content can distract from the main content and negatively impact the user experience. Consider omitting a footer on pages where minimalism and focus on primary content are more important than providing additional links and information, such as web applications or landing pages with a single call-to-action.
